SolidCraft roadmap: developer detail

The short, user-facing roadmap is ROADMAP.md. This page is the detailed developer status, kept as it was.

Status as of 2026-10-14. SolidCraft supports parametric modelling (sketch → features → parametric timeline) as an open, pure-Rust, agent-drivable application.

Scope (owner decision, 2026-10-07)

The issue batch adds preservation of blind pockets in through cuts, associative projected offsets and plastic face placements, XZ (+Y normal) with a v6 migration preserving older designs, interactive angle placement, file-picker sketch imports, Home/recent controls, toolbar overflow, and the remaining component-frame and assembly WIP work. The enclosure acceptance scenarios now include board resizes; STEP reopening is automated. See WIP reconciliation. Face-based Extrude Join (including outward Press Pull) now defaults to the picked body, so edge-touching assembly neighbours stay separate regardless of body order (#226).

In scope, in priority order:

  1. SOLID and SKETCH (now).
  2. Parameters / functional constraints (done: see parameters.*, expr.evaluate; unit-aware, cycles reported with their path, rename follows references, deletes list their users): user parameters with units and expressions (+-*/, ^, parentheses, sin/cos/sqrt/min/max/floor/ceil/round, unit conversion), model parameters d1, d2… for every dimension and feature input, references between parameters with a dependency graph and cycle detection, re-evaluation on change, comments, favourites, CSV/JSON import and export.
  3. Components and assemblies with joints (M9).
  4. SHEET METAL: flange, contour flange, bend, unfold/refold, flat pattern with DXF export, sheet metal rules (thickness, K-factor, bend radius) (M10).
  5. A small PLASTIC subset for enclosures, after sheet metal: Boss, Rib, Web, Lip/Groove, Snap Fit.

Deferred until further notice: MESH, FORM (T-splines), SURFACE beyond what solids need (scope proposed in #772), PCB / 3D PCB / Package, Render, Animation, Simulation, Manufacture/CAM, Drawings.

Interactive modelling

  • Modelled threads preview their cut before OK using the background worker on desktop. Browser users apply the cut with OK to avoid blocking on every edit.

  • Thread resolves a suggested metric size when a cylindrical face is picked. Diameter and pitch selectors expose the existing metric designation parameter; custom designations stay editable, and Automatic size returns to the face's suggestion. Cosmetic/modelled and full/partial-length options remain available. This does not add tolerance classes or left-hand threads.

  • New Body extrusion and face-offset previews show completed intermediate geometry while a drag continues, then catch up to the exact final value. Changes to the selected geometry, operation or feature still discard obsolete results. Distance arrows and their value boxes follow the current frame's input.

  • Large fine modelled threads use pitch-based helix resolution and tighter faceted reconstruction fallbacks, preserving thin crests and closed shells on large shafts.

Physical material data

  • Physical material card validation (#923/#922) provides typed SI mechanical and thermal data, per-property sources/conditions and isotropic/orthotropic stability checks through read-only material.properties.validate. It does not assign or persist properties, provide a solver or enable rendering; see the contract and staged follow-ups.

  • Directional elasticity (#994) queries the validated card in material-local axes through read-only material.properties.directional, with source/condition review. It does not assign body frames, predict structural behavior or enable a solver.

Library foundations (#785, #786, #787)

  • Local scalar libraries (#846) inspect/export bounded portable catalogs and copy exact selected appearance/material revisions into existing design definitions in one undo step. Appearance offers local loading/search/export; physical materials use existing commands. This slice is independent of the render stack and does not change the document format. See the contract and limits.

  • Proposed phases cover offline bundled/project/user packs, physical materials and appearances, editable wood mapping, and parameterized component/assembly families. Stable identity/revision, copied design definitions, provenance and resource limits apply from the first implementation. Existing material, appearance and standard-part commands remain the application paths. See the proposal for source/license review and acceptance gates. This is planning only: pack loading, texture rendering and expanded component families are not implemented by this entry.

Render foundation

  • Original starter appearances (#1722): 34 opaque recipes through existing commands, with six explicitly unavailable glass recipes and preserved legacy looks; see the contract.

  • Bounded linear film accumulation (#1696) supplies caller-admitted mean/variance storage and complete-pass views; actual progressive consumers remain separate. See the film contract.

  • Definition-local visual mapping (#1214) supplies a bounded numerical frame, physical repeat coordinates and a deterministic one-time grain-axis suggestion. It adds no saved record or texture consumer; see the mapping contract.

  • Explicit texture normalization (#1218) produces completed float RGBA with role-aware linear color/data semantics, bounded matrix/TRC ICC conversion and opt-in EXIF orientation. It retains high-precision raw samples and records lossy gamut clipping only when explicitly requested. It is not a renderer/resource consumer; see the supported policy and limits.

  • Opt-in opaque PBR (#1046) connects persisted surface parameters to the integrated product viewport and CPU PNG, using a fixed direct-light studio. Geometry and mass stay unchanged. HDRI, transmission and textures remain separate gates. See the consumer and limitations.

  • Combined integration is tracked in #842, with independent scalar libraries in #863 and richer PBR/material-card transport in #1086. See phase boundaries and validation gates. Render/library consumers use the merged asynchronous picker (#1127). Material-card UI (#1094), texture mapping and HDRI lighting remain explicit follow-ups.

  • R4a adds bounded CPU product-image jobs, cancellation and atomic PNG export through engine commands, the Render panel, CLI and persistent MCP. Immutable captures and results stay outside persistence/undo; native workers and cooperative browser tasks share the same work protocol. See jobs, limits and export.

  • R3 adds an overlay-free product scene and the integrated Render workspace, with runtime GPU host capabilities, per-document drafts and named camera/background controls. Design keeps its camera and drawing policy. Raster transparency remains approximate; PBR and environments are later slices. See workspace and scene contracts.

  • R2 adds bounded named render setups in document format 21 and the read/save render.settings.get/set commands. Proven render-only changes preserve geometry and prepared world data through undo/redo and checkpoint restore. This slice does not add a Render workspace, PBR, environments or image jobs; see settings and refresh policy.

  • Bounded PNG/JPEG texture admission (#900) validates in-memory sources and returns completed RGBA8 pixels with explicit color/data roles. It is an input utility only: ICC/16-bit normalization, local wood mapping, resource persistence and the renderer consumer remain separate work. See limits and licensed-file probes.

  • The initial opaque surface numerical contract for #1004 provides bounded linear color/metallic/roughness/IOR admission and an original tested direct-light BRDF. The numerical module alone advertises no capabilities; the installed consumer above provides rendering. See the model, evidence and remaining consumer gates.

  • A bounded cooperative Radiance RGBE input utility is available in L1 render; it does not yet enable HDRI/PBR rendering or a document import command. See the input contract and #813 for integration work.

  • R1 adds renderer-independent backend/profile metadata, strict request resolution and bounded image/scratch admission checks, exposed by the read-only render.backends command. It describes the current CPU raster snapshot path without evaluating the model. The legacy Design snapshot route remains unchanged. Later slices above provide the workspace/jobs and opaque PBR; HDRI and optional engines remain future work. See the contract and the phased proposal (#620).

Dimensioning and error reporting

  • Operation length fields share design-unit defaults and input evaluation, including secondary distances, thin walls, blend controls, sheet/plastic rules, joints and motion keys. Canvas handles and sketch previews use the same lengths as committed geometry. Existing stored millimetre values retain their size when edited in a design using another unit (#3271).

  • MCP execute accepts verbose: false, sharing the compact reply helper used by batch: body changes and created sketch IDs stay, while repeated lists are omitted. Both tools retain full replies by default.

  • Sketch Create groups drawing variants under Line, Rectangle, Circle, Arc, Slot, Polygon and Spline. Each open part remembers its last interactive choice per family across sketch sessions and document-tab switches; closing the part resets these UI choices.

  • Inscribed, circumscribed and edge polygon tools preview their actual outline before placement, including orientation and the selected side count.

  • Construction (X) preserves the active sketch drawing tool, picked anchors and inline dimension values, so line type can change while a shape is in progress.

  • Planar sketch construction previews and hover feedback keep the same dashed pattern as committed geometry; centerlines retain their dash-dot pattern.

  • Point has a default Sketch CREATE toolbar button alongside the existing drawing tools. Saved custom layouts are preserved; Reset Toolbar restores the default Point button.

  • Construction planes cannot take an origin-plane name (XY, XZ, YZ, Top, Front, Right). Plane arguments reject such a name when an older design's construction plane already has it. Use the other alias, the construction feature's numeric ID or an explicit plane; existing stored references keep their saved meaning. Section Analysis uses the same check.

  • Sketch text can be edited from a letter interior or outline's context menu or the Edit Text command. Interior selection requires every profile boundary to belong to the same text source; the editor highlights the whole source without replacing profile selections. Content, baseline X/Y, height and rotation update the original text source in one undo step; cancelling or rejecting an invalid value preserves the sketch. Position and height fields use the document units captured when editing starts; generated outlines remain associated with their text source, and untouched inputs preserve their saved internal values. Dragging a text outline moves the entire source in one undo step; Escape restores the gesture. Move/Copy on a selected text outline provides unit-aware X/Y distances and rotation about its baseline; copies keep an independent editable text source.

  • Repeating the initial point of a planar multi-point sketch drawing tool is neutral: the tool waits for a distinct point without a degenerate-shape error or history entry. Typed input and programmatic commands retain their validation.

  • Circular-pattern and angled-plane axes reject explicitly supplied malformed or expression origins instead of silently rotating about zero. Numeric origins remain in millimetres; associative axis expressions and construction-axis references remain unsupported.

  • Single-line and rectangle-side dimensioning supports selecting a line or its two endpoints. Type a value and press Enter to commit one undo step; rejected values remain editable and cancellation leaves the sketch unchanged. Fit keeps geometry clear of the Sketch Palette and navigation controls, including enlarged displays and nested pending placements.

  • Sketch Dimension takes a line and a circle or arc (#1803): it measures from the line to the circle's edge (the gap, 0 when tangent, or how far the edge reaches past a line that cuts it), drives it like any other dimension and keeps the circle on its side of the line.

  • Sketch Dimension takes a point and a circle or arc, or two circles or arcs (#4064): it measures to the circle's edge (from the point, or between the two edges: the gap, 0 when they touch, how far they overlap, or the narrowest gap between nested circles).

  • Sketch annotations, live drawing lengths and Measure use document units. Inches show three decimals and retain a meaningful fourth digit. Bare length dimension entry captures the current design unit; changing units leaves existing geometry and expressions intact. Component/canvas distance controls, sketch cursor coordinates, flat-pattern dimensions and pocket depths, interference volumes and parts-library lengths also use design units.

  • Curvature and Polygon constraints accept complete preselected operands. Fix/Unfix consumes complete point/curve selections consistently; mixed or ambiguous selections retain the picking workflow. Rejected constraints preserve selection and undo history.

  • Contextual error reporting: a Create Issue action beside feature errors opens a reviewable GitHub issue draft with the error, operation settings, build diagnostics and useful reproduction context. The draft is editable, with optional capture of the actual viewport and copy/save controls. Opening GitHub does not submit the issue. Help also exposes reporting for unexpected results that did not produce an error.

  • The header exposes Report a bug, including an accessible compact control on narrow windows. It opens the same editable report and screenshot review without changing the active model or a pending dimension.

  • Crash reports: every panic (in a command, in the kernel, anywhere else) and every unclean exit (abort, stack overflow, kill: found at the next start through a locked per-run command journal) leaves a local report with the backtrace, version and commit, the command and the design that reproduce it (solidcraft-cli crashes replay <id>). Repeats are counted, not rewritten; at most 20 reports, 50 MB and 90 days. The app shows them after a crash (Help > Crash Reports…) with a reviewed issue draft; agents read them through diagnostics.crash_reports, the MCP crash_reports tool or the CLI. Nothing is sent anywhere. See crash reports.

  • Agent chat (off by default, desktop): a side panel that drives a coding agent the user already has (Claude Code through its ACP adapter, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenCode, Grok Build, …) over the Agent Client Protocol, under the user's own login; SolidCraft stores no keys. The agent works through SolidCraft's MCP tools on a gated endpoint of its own: file actions wait for Allow or Deny, crash reports are off limits, 24 changes per message, one undo step per message, and the agent's whole process group ends with the panel or the app. Docks left or right, minimises to a strip. Grok Build discovery includes ~/.grok/bin and reuses the user's Grok login. See agent chat.

  • Command scripts from designs (#886): timeline.export_script (CLI solidcraft-cli script) writes a design's history as a script that eval and run replay into the same design — user parameters, units, sheet metal and plastic rules and materials as their own commands, then each feature through timeline.add with its ids, names and dimension parameters kept, so every feature kind replays exactly and a failing feature fails the same way. Known limitations

  • Scheduled and manual CI scan locked dependency advisories, bans and sources with cargo-deny. Vulnerabilities fail the scan; unmaintained crates warn. Local vendored patches require separate upstream review because advisory databases cannot version-match their path dependencies. Pull requests retain the existing licence checks.

  • Associative shell-face sketches require a unique certified planar source. Nested shell attachments and ambiguous coincident sources remain unsupported.

  • G2 fillets: exact quintic blends of straight edges between planar faces, with one planar end face at each end, including sloped ends (#2708). The UI and solid.fillet expose curvature (alias g2); the size is an equivalent circular setback. Curved faces/edges, multi-face ends and corner junctions remain unsupported.

  • Fillets and chamfers: constant, variable-radius and chord-length fillets; a radius per edge, rounds next to earlier rounds of another radius, and smooth corner patches where three rounds meet (a sphere for equal radii); perpendicular corner fillets after a closed ring of earlier rounds preserve the ring's radii and the enclosed planar face (#1557); mitres between separate rounds of the same radius keep their endpoint tangent, so a later fillet can select an upright and its adjoining bottom mitre as one chain (#2422); straight edges (convex or concave) between planar faces with perpendicular planar end faces (equal-distance chamfers also meet sloped planar end faces, including inner basin rims within the rim width); convex fillets also meet sloped planar ends and reflex corners around pocket rims, with unequal wall slopes and extensions confined to the face; connected equal-distance chamfers also trim each other across unequal sloped rim walls; curved chamfer chains with straight planar ends can terminate across two existing planar bevel faces, splitting their shared seam; picks on the same tangent chain are applied once (#1303); whole smooth loops of a planar face, picked whole or by one edge (tangent chain), including circles and walls at any angle (boss bases and tops, bores, pocket floors, plate outlines), loops with sharp corners between straight or mixed arc/line edges (straight blends meet in mitres; perpendicular curved walls use offset intersections, with D-rim removal checked within 0.1% of analytic offset slices), runs of neighbouring edges along a planar face's loop (two top edges of a box: mitred, ending square; with the edge between them too, the corner becomes a sphere octant), rounded corners rounded again (sphere octants, tori at concave corners); every edge of a convex planar body or of an extruded part; fillets inside patterns; closed chains of curved edges between any two smooth sides (a branch pipe on a main pipe), by a rolling ball (the blend a rational B-spline through exact arcs), closed or ending at planar faces square to the edges, where straight or curved cap edges are trimmed to the contacts (the straight edge between two overlapping extruded arcs); knife edges, where the two faces have opposite normals (a wall that runs out tangent into a face: the ball that fits there); an edge that runs out along an end face tangent to one side (the arc top edge of that wall), cut where the blend meets it; rounds that end in an earlier round on curved faces, in any order (the rounds of two edges at a vertex meet in a crease, as on a box), picked together or one after the other, except a round after a round of the edge between the wall and a flat face that it would run over; the three edges at a vertex of any smooth faces, of one radius, end in a piece of sphere. A limiting blend that collapses a side wall into coincident boundary curves is refused as too large rather than returning an unmeshable face (#1113).

  • Booleans: prisms along one direction (plates with holes, slots, patterns of them, equal sections stacked end to end) are combined exactly in 2D. Qualified axial cuts preserve annular holes with overrun cutters, including inward Press Pull after a joined extrusion; curved wall qualification rejects false transverse axes instead of filling holes. Bodies touching along one whole face (a part and its mirror image) are stitched; coincident planar faces are pushed apart when their neighbours are planes or cylinders. Other coincident curved configurations can still fail. Bounded containment classification preserves fully internal cavities and nested material islands independently of shell order, with STEP round-trip and material-membership regressions. Near-contact curved shells use the general boolean path rather than a containment shortcut. Spheres are built from six pole-free patches.

  • Mass properties come from fine tessellation (curved faces within ~1e-4 relative); triangles that cut through curved faces (trimmed faces after booleans) are split onto the surface first.
