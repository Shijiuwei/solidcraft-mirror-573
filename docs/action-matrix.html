Action matrix

The action matrix tries every ordinary action on every face, edge, vertex and body of the final bodies of a corpus of real designs, through the same commands and parameters the desktop sends, and records what works. tests/action_matrix/baseline.json is a ratchet: an action that passed there and fails now is a regression and fails the test. The test is apps/solidcraft-cli/tests/action_matrix.rs.

Running it

  • Every CI run (pull requests too) checks a fixed sample, one action in 40 by a stable hash of its name, on every design except the slowest to build (the SLOW list, and any design that takes more than 8 s to load in costs.json; action_matrix_sample, a few minutes).
  • Nightly (and by hand, Run workflow), the "Action matrix" jobs run the whole matrix in 16 shards (action_matrix_shard with ACTION_MATRIX_SHARD=k/16) of about equal cost, cut by tests/action_matrix/costs.json: what each design took on a runner. A design that takes more than ten minutes is split into slices (actions by a hash of their name, each slice loading the design again), and the pieces go largest first to the shard with the least work. A design not in costs.json yet counts as two minutes. Each shard uploads its outcomes and timings (artifact action-matrix-k); with all sixteen in one folder, cargo xtask action-matrix --from DIR --bless writes the baseline and costs.json from them without running anything. ACTION_MATRIX_SHARD=k/n ACTION_MATRIX_SHARD_LIST=1 prints the plan.
  • Determinism (nightly, "Action matrix determinism"): action_matrix_determinism runs one action in 8 of every design that takes at most five minutes twice on one runner and lists every action whose outcome changed; it fails when a family of pieces passes a different number of times.
  • Locally: cargo xtask action-matrix runs it whole (a few hours on one machine now; the nightly shards' artifacts and --from are quicker). Add --bless to rewrite the baseline after a fix (newly passing actions are listed when you don't), and --results FILE to keep every outcome with its message and the command that was sent. ACTION_MATRIX_FILTER=oracle/23 runs only the designs whose name contains it; ACTION_MATRIX_JOBS sets how many designs run at once. For a quick local look, ACTION_MATRIX_ACTIONS=edge.fillet,edge.chamfer tries only those actions (never bless such a run) and ACTION_MATRIX_VALIDITY=1 adds each passing action's body check (Body::validity) to the results. ACTION_MATRIX_UNFOLD=1 also unfolds and refolds every sheet metal body after each action that passed, recorded as <action>+unfold. To recheck one design on its own: ACTION_MATRIX_DESIGN=oracle/69-sm-tray ACTION_MATRIX_OUT=out.jsonl ACTION_MATRIX_ACTIONS=face.hole cargo test --profile ci -p solidcraft-cli --test action_matrix action_matrix_worker -- --exact --ignored (from apps/solidcraft-cli).

Each design runs in its own process, so an action that hangs (90 s without a result) or aborts is recorded as timeout or crash and the rest of the design still runs. An action that passes in the baseline and times out is listed but not judged (and stays passing when blessed): the slowest actions take 40 to 80 s on a busy runner and land either side of the limit from night to night. Loading a design, before its first action, may take up to 300 s (the largest take several times longer on a loaded CI runner than here). None did in this run.

Pieces that share a name (#2, ~3) are numbered by where they lie, and the numbers of pieces lying almost level can swap between runs (#569). The ratchet therefore counts passes per family of pieces (the name without its numbers) and samples whole families, so a swap is not a regression.

Each open cause below has a small reproduction among the ignored tests at the end of action_matrix.rs (cargo test --profile ci -p solidcraft-cli --test action_matrix -- --ignored lists which still fail; skip action_matrix_ to leave out the matrix itself).

The corpus

  • corpus/*: the old design files of apps/solidcraft-cli/tests/corpus.
  • examples/*, regressions/*: the scripts in examples/.
  • oracle/*: the 76 reference recipes, rebuilt (solidcraft-cli recipe).
  • demo/enclosure_steps: the electronics enclosure built over MCP; demo/pi-case: the Raspberry Pi 4 case of the 0.6 README demo, built through the desktop UI and saved by examples/demo/enclosure_ui_mcp.py --story pi-0.6 --save; demo/pi-case-0.8: the 0.8 README demo's case (--story pi), with a rounded rim, engraved text and a PETG material; demo/logo-up-down-join: the SolidCraft logo as it was reported in #954, one sketch extruded up and down and joined.
  • typical/*: tests/action_matrix/designs, parts made the ordinary way: a stacked extrude, a filleted and shelled box, a revolved knob, a plate with a counterbored hole patterned 3 × 2 and a chamfered outline, a sheet metal L bracket with holes, a join and a cut of three bodies, a loft on a plate, a sweep, and one sketch of squares drawn edge to edge extruded up and down and joined (up-down-join).

The actions

Each one runs on a scratch copy of the design. It passes when the command works, the timeline has no new errors, and any sketch it makes is attached to its face (healthy, not a legacy face sketch found again by a point). A face pick is what the desktop sends: the world body, the face index, the occurrence path and a point inside the face; never fixed.

The edit round: one pass in six also changes a driving parameter (the first user parameter, else a feature's, by 10%, one that the design itself survives) and checks the new feature still resolves; it is recorded as <action>+edit.

Where the face history stands (crates/kernel/src/history.rs, face attachments): a flat face takes an attached sketch when the body carries an exact source certificate for it (extrude caps and sides, boxes, shells' inner walls, STEP faces, sheet metal panels), or else when its persistent name follows exact history (a lineage certificate): cut walls and floors, chamfers, pattern and mirror copies, sides a Join merged, revolves, lofts, sweeps, drafts, tapers, shell rims, the plastic features' faces and the faces around them, and pieces of a split face (by the piece's own name where Offset Face moved it off the others' plane). Sheet metal panels, their rims and the flat ends of bends attach by name. What still refuses a sketch is what makes faces without recording their history (some cuts, the face a Split Body cuts with another body's face): press-pull and extrude-from-face then fall back to a legacy face sketch found again by a point.
